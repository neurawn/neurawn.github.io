## Deriving the RLHF Objective

**Policy** $$\pi_\theta(y \mid x)$$: probability that the model produces the full response $$y$$ given the prompt $$x$$. Since $$y$$ is generated token by token, this is a product of per-token probabilities:

$$
\pi_\theta(y \mid x) = \prod_t \pi_\theta(y_t \mid x, y_{<t})
$$

**Reward:** given a reward $$r(x, y)$$ for response $$y$$ (a reward model trained on human preference comparisons), the average (expected) reward is:

$$
\sum_{y} \pi_\theta(y \mid x)\, r(x, y) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)}\big[ r(x, y) \big]
$$

The expectation form is what makes this usable: there are far too many possible responses $$y$$ to sum over, but we can estimate the expectation by sampling responses from $$\pi_\theta$$.

### General Structure

$$
\text{Objective} = \text{avg predicted reward} - \beta \times \text{cost to change behavior}
$$

Why pay a cost at all? Without it, the model learns to exploit flaws in the reward model (reward hacking) and drifts away from fluent language. $$\beta$$ sets the trade-off: a larger $$\beta$$ keeps the model closer to the reference.

### Changing Behavior

$$
\frac{\pi_\theta(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)}
\quad
\begin{array}{l}
\leftarrow \text{our behavior} \\
\leftarrow \text{ref behavior}
\end{array}
$$

This tells us how much more ($$> 1$$) or less ($$< 1$$) likely the new policy is to produce $$y$$ than the reference.

- Take a log and average over all answers generated by the new model. This is the KL divergence:

$$
\sum_{y} \pi_\theta(y \mid x) \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)} = D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big( \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x) \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot \mid x) \big)
$$

The average is weighted by $$\pi_\theta$$, not $$\pi_{\text{ref}}$$, because we only have samples from our own model. This makes it the *reverse* KL, which tends to stick to the reference model's most likely answers. Because of the per-token product above, in practice the penalty is applied token by token.

### Putting It Together

Combining the two pieces and averaging over prompts $$x \sim \mathcal{D}$$:

$$
\max_\theta \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}} \Big[ \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)}\big[ r(x, y) \big] - \beta \, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big( \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x) \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot \mid x) \big) \Big]
$$

Since both terms are expectations over $$y \sim \pi_\theta$$, they fold into a single expectation, which is the form PPO-style RLHF optimizes:

$$
\max_\theta \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D},\; y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)} \Big[ r(x, y) - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)} \Big]
$$
